An introductory adventure for At the Last Throw

2–3 hours  3–4 Protagonists  At least one Fighter  Narrator’s edition

The four pre-generated character sheets will be provided in a separate PDF, one sheet per Protagonist, using the rulebook character-sheet template.

A Word to the Narrator

This adventure is a teaching tool first and a story second. Every scene foregrounds a specific pillar of At the Last Throw without ever pausing the fiction to lecture. By the time the credits roll, your players will have lived through tier rolls, narrative distances, the Wounds-and-Fatigue pool, the Helping mechanic, and — if you do your job — at least one Exertion declared with a shaky hand at exactly the moment the title promises.

The pacing target is roughly:

  • 30 minutes for character creation and setup (use the creation rules from the manual, pp. 4–8)

  • 35 minutes for Scene 1 · The Village (social investigation)

  • 40 minutes for Scene 2 · The Whispering Wood (skill challenge + atmosphere)

  • 60 minutes for Scene 3 · The Old Watchtower (combat climax + resolution)

  • 10 minutes for The Three Questions and aftermath

A reminder that bears repeating: in this game there are no initiative rolls, no fixed turn order, no grid. The maps included with this adventure are spatial anchors for everyones imagination — distances at the table are always Close, Near, In Range, or Far.

What This Adventure Teaches

|Y|Y &
The Village & Attribute + Skill rolls; TN for social actions; opposed rolls; Trait and Burden engagement
The Whispering Wood & Helping; Conditions (−1 tier); narrative distance bands
The Old Watchtower & Combat; Wounds and Fatigue; Critical; Backlash; Exertion; consequences

If the party includes a Mystic, Scene 3 is also where their first Backlash will likely occur — let it.

The Premise

Seven generations ago, a wandering Mystic named Veyl Ashen blessed a bronze bell cast in the forge of a small settlement built where the Kings road ends and the old forest begins. The settlement took the name Hollow Hearth. The bell, rung at every dusk, kept the Hollow Folk — pale, bark-skinned things that whisper from between the trees — back among the roots where they belong.

Three nights ago, the bell was stolen.

A band of brigands led by a deserter named "Sir" Corwin Vask descended from the watchtower ruins where they camp, killed the old bellringers apprentice, and vanished northward into the wood with the bell wrapped in oilcloth. The villagers buried the boy yesterday morning.

The next moonless night is in forty-eight hours.

That is the clock. After moonset on that night, with no bell to sound at dusk, the Hollow Folk will return to a Hollow Hearth that has forgotten how to defend itself.

The Hook

Tailor the arrival to the Traits and Burdens your players have written. Some quick prompts:

  • A Scholar tracking the lineage of Veyl Ashen through old liturgical records.

  • An Adventurer carrying a contract from the next town up the road.

  • A Mystic who felt the bell go silent from leagues away.

  • A Fighter walking off a battlefield they no longer want to be on.

Whatever the route, the village mayor, Hesta Thrush, will offer them what little Hollow Hearth has — coin, hospitality, and a relic kept since the founding (see Rewards) — to bring the bell home.

Read aloud — Arrival

The road thins to a track, the track to a footpath, and the footpath spills into a clearing of low stone houses with steep slate roofs. A wooden platform stands in the centre of the village square. Empty. Four iron bolts, where something heavy was wrenched away. There is no smoke from the chimneys. There is no one in the square.

Then a door opens, and an old woman steps out, and behind her you can hear someone weeping.
