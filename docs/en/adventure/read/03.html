Mechanic focus: Helping mechanic, distance bands, Conditions as -1 tier.

A path of crushed bracken and dropped oilcloth scraps leads north from the village. The Whispering Wood swallows it within fifty paces. The trees here lean inward.

Read aloud — Entering the Wood

The light drops a third the moment you cross the treeline. Not from the canopy — there is no canopy worth the name — but as if the air itself drinks it. Sound flattens. Footsteps stop carrying. After ten minutes of walking, the only thing you can hear is your own breath and, very far off, what could be wind in branches or could be voices saying something you cannot quite catch.

Refer to the Whispering Wood journey map for the layout of the journey from village to watchtower, with the bridge and the Glimpse waypoint marked.

The Trail · A Survival Challenge

The trail is real but easy to lose. A character with Survival rolls TN 4 Dexterity. A Dex Competent character with the skill rolls 2d6 keep highest; a Dex Expert without the skill rolls the same; a Dex Untrained with no skill rolls 2d6 keep lowest, which is unkind.

This is the perfect moment to introduce Helping (manual pp. 9–10):

  • A Competent helper — one whose own roll on the same task would be at Expert level or higher — adds +1d6 to the lead character’ s pool with no penalty.

  • An Incompetent helper — anyone who would roll below Expert level on the same task — adds +1d6 but raises the TN by one. A Fighter loudly "helping" the Adventurer track may make things worse.

Let the players watch this happen. A Fighter who helps the tracker turns TN 4 into TN 5 — and then realises the lesson when the trail is lost.

If the trail is lost, an extra hour passes and dusk catches the party in the wood. Everything from that point until sunrise rolls under a Condition: -1 tier.

The Rotted Bridge

The trail crosses a ravine on a single-log bridge that has not been maintained in twenty years. The log will hold one person at a time. Across is fifty feet down to a stream over rocks.

  • Dex roll, TN 4 to cross safely. With Acrobatics, comfortable.

  • A character who falls takes 2 Wounds (Combat — from the rocks). A Medicine roll, TN 4, in-scene, restores 1 Wound (2 on a Critical).

  • A rope strung across as a handhold (Strength + Craftsmanship or + Endurance, TN 3) lowers everyone’ s TN to 3 and removes the consequence of failure.

This is also a quiet test of Trait. A Protagonist whose Trait reads "I leave no one behind" who fails their own crossing roll — and is then helped across by a companion — should feel that.

The Glimpse

Whether the party crosses well or badly, as the last person reaches the far side, a Hollow person appears between two trees on the side they just left. Pale. Bark-skinned. Slightly too tall. Mouth not quite where a mouth should be. It does not move. It does not threaten. It watches them walk away. After thirty seconds, it is gone.

This is atmosphere only. There is no roll. Let the players sit with it.

Teaching moment — Distances

The Hollow person is Far (across the ravine, beyond reasonable bow range). A Mystic with a sensing Grimoire — Eye of the Void (manual p. 14), for example — might want to look closer. Let them. Make them pay the Fatigue cost. Show the table what a Wonder roll looks like when the stakes are only knowledge.

The Wolves · Optional Scaling Encounter

If the session is moving fast and the party seems undertested, a pair of starved wolves can ambush them in the last stretch before the watchtower. Two wolves teach the group enemy rule (manual p. 16) and let the Fighter swing for the first time.

Starved Wolf (optional Wood encounter) Competent · 2 Wounds · bite (2W Str). Behaviour: "Hunts the weakest. Three nights without food."
