Mechanic focus: Combat as conversation, Wounds vs Fatigue, Critical, Backlash, Exertion.

The Old Watchtower is two storeys of pitted stone on a low rise, half its roof gone, ivy in the cracks. A campfire glows in front of the entrance. Two brigands sit by it. Inside the lower floor, two more sleep. On the upper floor, Sir Corwin Vask sits with the bell wrapped in oilcloth, waiting for his buyer.

The buyer is due at midnight. It is dusk now.

Refer to the Old Watchtower ground-floor map for the ground floor and approaches, and the Old Watchtower upper-floor map for the upper floor layout where the climax takes place.

Brigands

Brigand TN S3/D3 · Competent · 2 Wounds · dagger (2W Dex) or short club (1F Str). Behaviour: "Cowards in a fair fight. Flee if Corwin falls." As a group: roll once, +1W per member beyond the first (max +3). Each Wound dealt to the group eliminates one member.

Three Approaches

The party has options. Before any rolls, ask plainly: "How do you want to do this?" The mechanics shift accordingly.

Stealth. The collapsed wall on the west side can be spotted on a Lore + Tactics roll, TN 3, or simple description. Each character rolls Dex + Stealth, TN 4 to approach unseen. A failure alerts the two brigands at the campfire. Two failures alert everyone, including Corwin. A clean approach lets the party reach the upper floor with surprise on him.

Parley. Walk up to the campfire and talk. Corwin is a man of broken pride. If a Protagonist (especially the Fighter) addresses him as "Sir Corwin," his Burden cracks. Lore + Diplomacy or Rhetoric, TN 5 convinces him to consider selling the bell back rather than to the buyer. He will demand more than the village’s reward — but the party can promise it. Lying to him about the reward is a Lore + Deception roll opposed by his Lore (Competent).

Assault. Walk in fighting. Two brigands at the fire, two inside, Corwin upstairs. Loud, bloody, and the cleanest way to teach Combat to new players.

Sir Corwin Vask, Disgraced Knight TN S4/D5 · Expert · 6 Wounds · longsword (2W Str) · heavy armour. Behaviour: "Will not strike a man on his knees. Will duel anyone who calls him ‘sir’. Hates Mystics — his order taught him to." Optional: he carries a battered locket with a portrait of his sister inside. If a Protagonist sees it during the fight, give them a chance to use it.

The Upper Floor

The bell, Sir Corwin, and the climactic confrontation are all on the upper floor. Its layout matters because the tactical choices the players make depend on it.

Sir Corwin sits on a wooden chair beside the south window, watching the path the buyer will come up. The bell sits beside him on a small table, still wrapped in muddied oilcloth. A brazier of low coals provides the only warm light in the room. The stairs come up into the north wall — behind him.

The western half of the upper floor is open sky: the same collapse that took out the western wall on the ground floor took out the western roof and part of the western flooring. Loose beams hang at angles. The exposed western flooring is unsafe to walk across without care.

This creates a fourth approach that the earlier text only hinted at:

The Climb. A character with Acrobatics can climb the rubble pile of the collapsed wall on the ground floor and continue up the outside of the broken western wall, entering through the gap in the roof. Roll Dex + Acrobatics, TN 4 to climb cleanly; without Acrobatics, TN 5. A failure means falling — 1 Wound from the height plus the noise of the fall (Corwin will hear). On a success, the character drops onto the upper floor behind Corwin, with surprise. This is a single-character approach: the Fighter in heavy armour cannot make this climb, and the Mystic with 2 HP cannot afford the fall on a failure. A nimble Adventurer is best suited to this approach.

Tactical features of the upper floor:

  • The south window. Anyone reaching the bell can hurl it out — it lands hard near the campfire below, possibly cracked. The buyer, watching from the trail, will see it. A Protagonist can also use the window to escape the room, dropping to the ground (Dex + Acrobatics, TN 4 to land cleanly; failure costs 1 Wound).

  • The unsafe western half. Combat that takes place on the collapsed half rolls under a Condition: −1 tier. A character forced over the edge falls one storey (2 Wounds). Corwin, who knows the room, will fight only on the eastern half — and will gladly try to push enemies onto the bad footing.

  • The brazier. Tipped over, embers scatter onto the dry wood floor — fire spreads in two scenes’ worth of action, and the room becomes uninhabitable. A desperate move, available to anyone, that may save the party or destroy the bell along with everything else.

  • Sir Corwin’s heavy armour. His heavy armour sets the TN to hit him to 4 with Strength or 5 with Dexterity. It does not reduce damage. A tipped brazier, a push toward the collapsed edge, or a Critical from a Protagonist who declared Exertion can change the confrontation.

The Twist · The Buyer

If the party is still at the watchtower at midnight — whether mid-fight, talking, or having won — a single robed figure walks out of the wood, alone, faceless under the hood. The buyer is a Hollow person in mortal guise. They want the bell silenced forever, not destroyed. They have a small bag of silver. They speak in the same not-quite-right voices the party half-heard on entering the wood.

The Buyer (Hollow Person in mortal guise) TN S3/D4 · Expert · 4 Wounds · touch (1F Wonder; on Critical, 2W + a Condition that lasts the rest of the scene) · no armour. Behaviour: "Speaks the wrong syllables. The trees do not answer. Bleeds sap, not blood."

This is a moment of choice. The party can:

  • Refuse and fight. (Stat block above.)

  • Accept the silver. The party walks away rich. Hollow Hearth is not seen alive again. Let this be a real ending if they choose it.

  • Lie and ambush the buyer. Opposed Lore rolls vs the buyer’s Lore (Expert). If the party wins, they keep the silver and the bell — but the wood has marked them, and the next adventure begins with that mark.

The Climax — and the Last Throw

Whatever path is chosen, the climactic moment should leave at least one Protagonist in Battered or Severe state. Watch the HP totals at the table. The instant a player drops to 1 HP and still has a roll to make to finish the scene — pause. Look at them across the table. Ask:

"It’s at the last throw. Do you want to bleed for it?"

Walk them through declaring Exertion (manual p. 13). Let them roll; the Fatigue Wound comes after the roll. This is the moment the game is named for. Make it feel like it. If the roll succeeds, the room should cheer. If it fails, the room should fall quiet.

Resolution

Three clean endings, with consequences that seed the next adventure:

The Bell Returns. Hesta awards the Blade of Salt and the village’s full hospitality. If her flower was brought back, Vela counts the party among her friends. Word spreads to the next town. The next adventure begins with the party being sought out by name.

The Bell is Silenced or Sold. The party leaves rich and marked. The Hollow Folk note them. A future adventure begins with whispers in the night that know their names.

The Bell is Destroyed. Whether by an unlucky Backlash or a desperate fight, the Hollow Folk return to Hollow Hearth on the moonless night. The next adventure begins with the party trying to undo it. Veyl Ashen, the original Mystic, may not be as dead as the histories say.

The Three Questions

End the session by having the group answer the three Advancement questions together (manual p. 11). An honest accounting — not a generous one — is the right one. A new player who blocked at every turn earned no advancement points. A new player who tried, failed, and reckoned with consequence earned three. Make this clear, gently, the first time.
